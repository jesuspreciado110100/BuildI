3 · TRUST

When things go wrong

A key people can't rely on is worse than a metal one. These are the questions buyers will ask, and how the Home Key answers each.

Battery
"My phone died at the door."

Wallet keys on most recent phones keep working for several hours after the battery runs out. The unit's smart lock also takes a PIN code as backup, and the lobby has a staffed desk.

Signal
"There's no signal in the parking garage."

The key works over NFC, directly between phone and reader. No internet needed. Changes (new guests, revocations) sync the next time the phone is online.

Lost phone
"I lost my phone last night."

Revoke from any device with the BuildI account, or call the building admin. A new key is issued to the new phone after an ID check. The access log shows if the lost phone was used.

Selling
"I sold my apartment."

When the sale is registered, the owner's keys and every guest key they issued are revoked at once, and the new owner gets their own Home Key with the unit's history.

Renting out
"I want to rent it out."

Investors issue a tenant key with the lease dates. It expires automatically at the end of the lease, and the owner keeps no access to the unit door during it.

Power cut
"What if the power goes out?"

Smart locks run on batteries for months, and every door has a mechanical key override kept by the admin. Emergency exits always open from inside.

Guests
"My grandmother doesn't have a smartphone."

Guest keys can also be a printed QR code or a PIN valid for set hours. Nobody is left outside because of the phone they own.

Privacy
"Who sees when I come and go?"

Only the owner sees their unit's door log. The admin sees common-door events without names unless there's a security incident, following the building's privacy policy.

4 · WHAT THE KEY MAKES POSSIBLE

Four more ideas built on the key

Once every door knows who you are, new services become simple.

The keyring disappears

On key day, the advisor hands over the metal backup keys in a sealed envelope "for emergencies only". Everything day to day is the card. A small ceremony that shows buyers what's different.

Investor buyers issue a tenant key tied to the lease. It starts and ends with the contract, and handing the unit back doesn't require changing a lock.

Package lockers that know you

Couriers drop packages in the lobby's lockers. The owner's key opens only their locker, and the app tells them which one. No lost deliveries, no waiting for the doorman.

What to decide next

Three things make the Home Key real: choosing an access-control provider that supports Apple Wallet and Google Wallet keys in Mexico, specifying the readers and smart locks in the building design before construction starts, and agreeing who runs the console (the builder until delivery, the building administration after). With those settled, the card can ship on day one of sales and the doors on key day.
